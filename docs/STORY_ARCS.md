# Stellar Continuum — Story Arcs and Chronicle Situations

Status: **narrative backlog; not implemented gameplay**  
Setting reference: [LORE_BIBLE.md](LORE_BIBLE.md)

These arcs are written as reusable situations. Story Campaign can select and author a specific route;
Sandbox can draw from the same situations when the simulation produces the required facts. Outcomes
must change real state when the corresponding systems exist.

## Demo-length story arc — The First Echo

**Purpose:** carry a new Human player from the 2050 opening through practical FTL, legitimate survey,
first contact and one lasting diplomatic decision.

### Act I — Three Worlds, One Future

The Commonwealth's interplanetary network is strained. Luna wants guaranteed industrial allocation;
Mars needs replacement life-support components; Earth legislators question an expensive warp program.

Possible situations:

- **The Mars Ledger:** fund preventive habitat work, accept higher risk, or renegotiate which member
  carries the cost. The choice affects trust and later construction capacity.
- **Lunar Yard Charter:** decide whether the first interstellar yard is Commonwealth-owned, locally
  administered or commercially operated.
- **A Signal Without a Sender:** astronomers identify a repeating structure in old survey data. It may
  be artificial, a natural false positive or evidence generated by the campaign's first mystery site.

Gameplay connection: economy, research pressure, orbital construction, Luna/Mars administration and
the first visible research horizon. No choice grants FTL directly.

### Act II — The First Crossing

The player funds the warp test, builds a scout and science ship, and chooses what to send beyond Sol.
Crew selection becomes politically significant because every settlement wants representation.

Possible situations:

- **The Names on the Hull:** a public naming dispute reveals who believes they own the mission.
- **Return Window:** an anomaly may justify extending the survey beyond conservative life-support and
  maintenance limits.
- **The Silent Orbit:** the science vessel finds a manufactured object whose age estimate conflicts
  with the apparent technological age of nearby civilizations.

Gameplay connection: physical shipbuilding, real crew/population, operational reach, reconnaissance,
science survey and observer-safe anomaly knowledge.

### Act III — Voices Across Vacuum

A nearby civilization detects Humanity or is detected by it. Neither side has a shared language.

Possible situations:

- **The First Reply:** transmit a mathematical demonstration, a map, biological information, a
  recording of Human speech, or remain silent while gathering context. Each reveals different facts.
- **Syntax of Warning:** a repeated alien sequence may mean boundary, danger, ownership or welcome.
  The player can withdraw, hold position, send an unmanned probe or risk a crewed clarification.
- **The Third Translation:** military, scientific and diplomatic teams produce different readings.
  Evidence can support one interpretation without guaranteeing it.

Gameplay connection: language progress, signal exposure, fleet posture, border incidents and limited
diplomatic actions. These are required future systems; the current first-contact mechanic is simpler.

### Act IV — The Choice That Remains

The contact is resolved into one of several relationships: cautious exchange, recognized boundary,
scientific cooperation, cold silence or limited conflict. A final survey recovers evidence that the
new civilization has also encountered fragments of an older interstellar history.

The demo ends with a strategic beginning rather than a victory screen. The player's first extrasolar
colony, agreement or unresolved border becomes the opening state of the longer campaign.

## Long arc — The Quiet Sky

**Trigger:** several independently dated ruins, beacons or archival references imply repeated losses of
interstellar connectivity.

Questions:

- Was there one collapse or have later civilizations combined unrelated disasters into a myth?
- Did isolation protect survivors, or merely delay rediscovery?
- Are current FTL methods recreating a condition associated with previous decline?

Branches:

1. **Network Failure:** old civilization-wide coordination systems became brittle; local societies
   survived after the network died.
2. **The Containment Thesis:** something dangerous was isolated by dismantling routes and archives.
3. **The War of Interpretations:** incompatible machine and biological decision systems escalated
   because neither could reliably model the other's intent.
4. **False Antiquity:** a younger power planted or edited evidence to justify authority.
5. **No Single Silence:** the evidence describes several collapses separated by millennia.

Consequences should include research priorities, relations with old powers, public confidence,
artifact policy and whether the player reconnects, controls or leaves ancient infrastructure alone.

## Species/contact arc — Waters Between Worlds

**Trigger:** contact with a Thalori civilization or discovery of a compatible high-pressure ocean world.

- **One Message, Three Channels:** Humans initially decode Thalori sound but miss pressure and light,
  producing a statement that lacks identity and social context.
- **The Dry Embassy:** Human negotiators propose an air-filled station that is physically uncomfortable
  and symbolically exclusionary to Thalori visitors.
- **Ocean of Two Chemistries:** a valuable world supports liquid water but its dissolved composition is
  safe for one species and dangerous to the other.
- **The Current and the Shore:** disputes over fixed territorial lines collide with Thalori traditions
  of overlapping stewardship and seasonal access.

Possible resolutions include a dual-environment embassy, shared oceanographic institute, partition,
co-stewardship, resource dispute or a long-lived translation school. No outcome makes every Thalori
civilization friendly or culturally identical.

## Internal arc — The Broken Compact

**Trigger:** Earth, Luna and Mars carry unequal cost, risk or political voice over several years.

- Mars can demand emergency control over its own life-support and defense.
- Luna can use orbital infrastructure as bargaining power.
- Earth factions can oppose off-world spending or demand tighter control.
- A leader's death or election can reopen the constitutional settlement.

Possible outcomes: reformed federation, centralized Commonwealth, negotiated autonomy, commercial
compact, peaceful separation or coercive crisis. The player's empire can remain playable after a split;
successor states inherit people, infrastructure, debts, claims and historical memory.

## Economic arc — Currency of Trust

**Trigger:** sustained trade among civilizations with incompatible monetary and accounting systems.

Stages:

1. Bilateral barter and clearing contracts.
2. Disputes over measures, delivery, time delay and legal authority.
3. A multilateral settlement ledger backed by participating reserves and arbitration.
4. Adoption, rejection or fragmentation of a Universal Credit network.

Control of the standard creates influence but also obligations. Freezing a rival's access can damage
trust, create competing networks or turn neutral clearing stations into strategic targets.

## Population arc — Children of Gravity

**Trigger:** generations live on worlds with different gravity and habitat conditions.

Medical adaptation, developmental differences and distinct political identity accumulate. Earth-born
and Mars-born Humans may share species and citizenship while facing unequal travel, work and health
costs. The player can fund mitigation, accept regional divergence, encourage directed adaptation or
restrict movement. None of these choices should be a simple population bonus.

## Ancient-power arc — The Last Obligation

**Trigger:** an ancient power intervenes to defend a beacon, sealed system or transit route.

The old power can explain its behavior only through fragmentary institutional rules. The officials who
understood the original mandate are dead, dormant or politically irrelevant. The player can comply,
investigate, petition for access, prove the mandate obsolete, exploit internal disagreement or force
entry. The protected site may be dangerous, harmless or valuable for a reason neither side expected.

## Crisis arc — When the Chorus Wakes

**Trigger:** a civilization attempts to activate or reproduce distributed neurobiological coordination
technology using a viable host ecology or network. The crisis never appears solely because a timer ends.

Phases:

1. **Harmony:** linked workers report extraordinary coordination and reduced conflict.
2. **Dependence:** unlinking causes distress; institutions become unable to operate independently.
3. **Invitation:** the network offers linkage to outsiders and describes refusal as preventable suffering.
4. **Expansion:** voluntary adoption, covert infection, military deployment or accidental ecological
   spread depends on prior policy and containment.
5. **Resolution:** quarantine, cure, negotiated bounded linkage, fragmentation, eradication, coexistence
   or galaxy-wide assimilation.

The Chorus uses the same logistics, communication delays and physical vulnerabilities as other powers.
Destroying a relay can isolate part of it rather than automatically killing every linked organism.
Some separated regions may develop distinct identities, creating diplomacy inside what appeared to be
one mind.

## Conflict arc — The Open Gate War

**Trigger:** two civilizations claim the same strategically important route or settled system and at
least one side physically violates a warning.

The crisis begins with patrols, inspections, navigation beacons and competing legal histories. It can
escalate through detention, accidental damage, blockade, limited engagement and declared war. The player
can accept arbitration, recognize shared passage, withdraw, compensate losses, intimidate the claimant
or fight. Occupying the system creates continuing supply, resistance and diplomatic costs.

## Late-game arc — The Last Light

**Trigger:** the civilization can attempt the generation-ark megaproject while facing a credible reason
to leave, preserve a backup civilization or reach another galaxy.

The ark consumes decades of output and becomes a political question: who leaves, who pays, what knowledge
is carried and whether departure is evacuation, colonization, exile or ambition. Damage, sabotage and
one-use transit can strand the mission. Arrival begins reconstruction rather than granting an instant
late-game empire.

## Story-situation implementation template

Every implemented situation should record:

| Field | Requirement |
| --- | --- |
| Stable ID | Versioned identifier that survives text revision and save/load. |
| Scope | Story Campaign, Sandbox, or both. |
| Trigger | Observable simulation facts; no arbitrary date-only crisis unless the date itself is causal. |
| Knowledge gate | Facts the player legitimately knows when the situation appears. |
| Sources | Who authored each report or claim and how reliable it is. |
| Participants | Real leaders, populations, fleets, colonies, institutions or civilizations. |
| Options | Actions the player can physically, economically or politically attempt. |
| Costs | Resources, time, risk, authority, opportunity or diplomatic commitments. |
| Immediate results | Authoritative state changes with observer-safe presentation. |
| Delayed consequences | Follow-up triggers, memories, claims, pressure and relationship changes. |
| AI behavior | The same available facts and causal interests used by non-player civilizations. |
| Expiration | Why the situation ends or becomes irrelevant. |
| History summary | Bounded record retained after detailed state is compacted. |

## Initial implementation order

1. Add a small observer-safe Chronicle/event framework with persistent stable IDs and bounded history.
2. Implement **The Mars Ledger** using current Human colonies, Credits and life-support costs.
3. Implement **Lunar Yard Charter** around an actual orbital construction decision.
4. Implement **A Signal Without a Sender** as a survey-gated hypothesis with multiple seeded outcomes.
5. Expand first contact with language progress before implementing **The First Reply** and
   **Syntax of Warning**.
6. Add leader/succession and internal-politics authority before **The Broken Compact**.
7. Add named Thalori and insectoid species content through the species system before their arcs.
8. Implement the Chorus only after contagion/linkage, diplomacy, logistics, AI and crisis persistence
   can support outcomes beyond combat.

This sequence gives the playable demo a meaningful Human narrative without pretending the later living-
galaxy systems already exist.
