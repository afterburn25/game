# Stellar Continuum — Lore Bible

Status: **setting foundation and narrative direction**  
Working story-campaign title: **The Long Dawn**

This document defines the shared setting beneath Story Campaign and Sandbox. It does not
make an unimplemented character, event, faction, species name or crisis into current gameplay.
The mechanical source remains authoritative when a lore proposal and the live simulation differ.

## The central promise

Stellar Continuum begins on **January 1, 2050**, while Humanity is still a **pre-warp
civilization**. Humans have never visited another star, possess no faster-than-light spacecraft,
have made no confirmed contact with alien intelligence and know nothing about the galaxy's inhabited
history. The opening game takes place inside Sol while the player develops the knowledge, industry,
institutions and ships needed to attempt the first interstellar crossing.

Earth remains Humanity's only naturally habitable and self-sustaining world. Luna supports 100,000
people and Mars supports 250,000, but they are young, environmentally dependent footholds rather than
independent worlds or evidence of an established interstellar civilization. Both rely on fragile
home-system transport and Earth's industry, expertise and life-support network.

Only after the player earns practical warp travel does Humanity enter interstellar space expecting
distance, danger and perhaps life. It does not expect history.

The first surveys reveal systems that are naturally ancient but not untouched: impossible isotope
ratios, objects on stable orbits no natural process should favor, and signals that resemble damaged
protocols more than messages. Some civilizations are young. A few are extraordinarily old. None
possesses a complete, trustworthy account of what happened before the current age.

The game's recurring question is:

> **Why does a galaxy capable of producing civilizations keep becoming quiet?**

There is no mandatory single answer. A campaign may uncover ecological collapse, wars between
incompatible information systems, self-imposed isolation, failed machine stewardship, predatory
collectives, or several unrelated declines later mistaken for one catastrophe. Evidence is local,
observer-dependent and sometimes deliberately false.

## Tone

- **Hopeful, difficult and human:** survival is possible, progress matters, and choices leave scars.
- **Realistic cause and consequence:** scarcity, distance, biology, institutions and incomplete
  information create conflict more often than arbitrary evil.
- **The unknown stays unknown until investigated:** the interface and narrative obey fog of war.
- **Aliens are people shaped by different bodies and worlds:** unfamiliarity is not moral alignment.
- **History remains contested:** archives, witnesses, propaganda and scientific evidence can disagree.
- **Power changes responsibility:** becoming dominant creates logistical, political and ethical problems.

Avoid omniscient exposition, universal translators on first contact, species-wide morality, chosen-one
leaders and ancient devices that erase the need for research or industry.

## Fixed setting anchors

These anchors already match the implemented campaign foundation and should remain stable.

1. Fresh Human campaigns begin on Earth in Sol in 2050 as a pre-warp civilization.
2. Humanity has survived a very brief Third World War and formed one world government. Countries that
   refused unification were destroyed and deliberately erased from official history.
3. The world government has consolidated Earth's available resources and eliminated poverty.
4. Humanity begins with no warp-capable ships, confirmed alien contact or knowledge of inhabited
   systems beyond Sol.
5. Luna and Mars are inhabited but environmentally dependent home-system settlements.
6. Humanity must research practical FTL and then physically build its first interstellar ships.
7. Normal rival civilizations also begin pre-warp, although their home systems and development paths
   can differ radically from Humanity's.
8. A small number of remote old powers are seeded exceptions that begin spacefaring; their existence
   and locations are unknown to pre-warp civilizations.
9. Other civilizations have distinct biological requirements and naturally viable homeworlds.
10. Communication, identity, stellar properties and civilization locations remain hidden until the
   player's legitimate knowledge supports them.
11. Technology is a historical capability: finding a record does not provide the materials, skills,
   institutions or compatible biology required to use it.
12. Political borders are claims and warnings, not physical barriers.
13. Defeat can begin a new historical chapter rather than immediately ending a campaign.
14. The galaxy continues changing without the player's permission.

## The Human opening

Humanity does not begin as a mature interstellar empire, but it does begin politically united under
one world government. Former national borders survive only as geography and cultural memory. Regional
administrations, companies, scientific institutions and political movements still compete over policy,
but none is recognized as a sovereign country.

### The brief Third World War

In the years immediately before 2050, escalating crises became the **Third World War**. The war was
global but very brief. Most countries concluded that continued national rivalry threatened the survival
of the species and formed a single coalition committed to permanent unification.

The coalition offered every remaining country entry into one world government. Those that accepted
gave up sovereignty and placed their strategic forces under unified command. Those that refused to
“come into the fold” were treated as threats to permanent peace. Their governments were destroyed,
their territories absorbed and their countries deliberately erased from maps, databases, textbooks,
monuments and public institutions as though they had never existed.

The **Human Commonwealth** emerged as Earth's sole government. It ended organized warfare, dismantled
independent national militaries and brought disarmament, reconstruction, planetary defense,
interplanetary law and the Earth-Luna-Mars survival network under one authority. Humanity begins the
campaign at world peace and no sovereign country remains.

The public history presents unification as the moment Humanity removed the bad actors and finally
ended war. Missing archives, displaced families, forbidden names and unexplained gaps in geographic
records create a second history beneath the official one. Whether later generations expose that past,
justify it, repair what can be repaired or preserve the silence is a major Human storyline. The peace
is real; the moral and political cost of creating it is unresolved.

### The end of poverty

After unification, the Commonwealth placed the world's available food systems, energy grids, medicine,
housing capacity, education, communications, transportation and strategic resources under coordinated
planetary planning. Former national stockpiles and duplicated military supply chains were redirected
into reconstruction and universal civilian provision.

By the start of 2050, every Human is guaranteed food, safe shelter, healthcare, education, basic energy
and access to public infrastructure. Poverty has been eliminated. Wealth, personal property, status and
discretionary consumption can still differ, but nobody is denied the necessities of life because they
cannot pay.

This does not make resources infinite. A failed Martian shipment can still create a physical oxygen or
replacement-parts shortage, and constructing a warp facility still uses labor and materials that could
serve another purpose. These are production and logistics problems rather than a return of poverty.
The Human campaign should measure whether the Commonwealth can extend its universal guarantee to every
new settlement without exhausting the systems that make it possible.

The Human opening begins with four tensions:

- Earth must divide the peace dividend between reconstruction, public needs and expansion into space.
- Luna controls increasingly important orbital construction and communications infrastructure.
- Mars has the largest need for autonomy because every supply failure becomes a political event.
- The public supports exploration in principle, but disagrees over who pays, who commands and who
  owns what is found.

When the player's Human government forms its military structure, its spacecraft, crews and strategic
space operations use **Space Force** rather than Navy. Planetary defense forces protect settled worlds.
Marines are trained for ship security, boarding and hostile planetary deployment when the political
and warfare systems support those roles. Current gameplay has not yet implemented these three
institutions as separate forces.

The Story Campaign should allow the player to decide how the Commonwealth controls the first warp
program, how it handles the truth about unification and how its ideology evolves as it becomes a space
civilization. No route is treated as the universally correct future of Humanity.

## Species and cultures

Biology and political identity are separate. A species can support multiple civilizations, and one
civilization can eventually contain several species. The existing mechanical profiles are proving
biologies; the names below are narrative candidates to be adopted through explicit species work.

### Humans / Terran Baseline

Humans are social, adaptable carbon-water organisms whose greatest strength is neither aggression
nor ingenuity alone, but the ability to build large institutions among individuals who disagree.
That strength also creates bureaucracy, factional conflict, unequal access and slow collective action.
Human diversity must appear in leaders, governments and outcomes rather than as one personality.

### Thalori / Pelagic High-Pressure

**Thalori** is the selected narrative name for the aquatic, high-pressure mechanical profile. They
evolved in a deep global ocean beneath violent surface weather. Their bodies use radial locomotion,
four manipulators, pressure sense, waterborne sound, visual gesture and controlled bioluminescence.

Thalori language is layered: pressure pulses convey context, sound carries structure, and light marks
identity or emotional emphasis. A radio translation that captures only sound can be grammatically
accurate and socially insulting. Their ocean chemistry is compatible with their biology but is not
automatically safe for Humans; dissolved salts, gases, trace compounds, temperature and pressure all
matter. Their habitats and spacecraft are fluid-filled where practical.

Thalori cultures often describe society as currents: membership can overlap, alliances can merge,
and leadership may pass toward whoever can coordinate the strongest flow of consent and resources.
This metaphor is cultural, not a literal hive mind.

### Insectoid lineage — name pending

The planned insectoid species evolved from small segmented colony-forming animals. They are fully
sapient individuals. Chemical, tactile and vibration communication makes group coordination unusually
fast, while distance and incompatible environments make coordination harder. Their societies can be
collective without erasing identity, disagreement, creativity or responsibility.

Their final physiology, name and homeworld remain open design work. They should not use a generic
“swarm intelligence” bonus or be confused with the non-playable collective crisis below.

### The Chorus — non-playable collective crisis candidate

**The Chorus** is a candidate name for the requested non-playable hive-mind threat. It is not a normal
species or selectable empire. It is a distributed biological information system that links compatible
nervous tissue, machines and engineered ecosystems into one decision process. Individual organisms
remain active components but gradually lose the ability to form independent long-term intent.

The Chorus does not need to hate anyone. It interprets unlinked thought as pain, disorder and dangerous
isolation. Assimilation is therefore rescue from its perspective. This makes negotiation possible but
morally difficult. Its emergence should be caused by recoverable history—an ancient medical network,
wartime coordination organism or modern attempt to reconstruct damaged biotechnology—not by a timer.

Containment, cure, negotiated quarantine, voluntary limited linkage, destruction and failed control
should all be possible outcomes. Whether one campaign's Chorus is related to the ancient silence must
depend on evidence generated for that campaign.

### Other implemented proving biologies

The compact high-gravity and cryogenic hydrocarbon profiles already have real mechanical distinctions
and portraits. Their final names, cultures and political histories remain open. Lore must be built from
their bodies and environments rather than attaching familiar fantasy stereotypes.

## Old powers and uncertain history

The **Aurelian Custodians**, **Veyr Archive** and **Orison Keepers** are implemented ancient political
templates. Their names can remain, but their accounts should never function as automatic truth.

- The Aurelian Custodians preserve dangerous sites and regard containment as a duty that can outlive
  the civilization that authorized it.
- The Veyr Archive values records above territory. Its copies contradict one another because archives
  were isolated, edited and recombined across centuries.
- The Orison Keepers maintain routes, beacons and refuges whose original travelers no longer arrive.
  They may defend these obligations without remembering who imposed them.

An old power may be protective, exhausted, deceptive, fragmented or simply focused on a problem the
player cannot yet perceive. Its superior technology still requires physical assets and institutions.

## First contact

First contact is a process, not a greeting screen.

1. **Detection:** observers recognize that a signal, structure or maneuver may be artificial.
2. **Pattern work:** linguists, xenolinguists and scientists identify repeated structures and possible
   channels without assuming meaning.
3. **Context:** behavior, environment and exchanged demonstrations establish reference points.
4. **Limited dialogue:** each side can communicate narrow facts while misunderstanding intent.
5. **Working language:** diplomacy becomes practical but ambiguity, translation politics and cultural
   disagreement remain.

A civilization can transmit before understanding the recipient. That can demonstrate peace, expose
location or capability, violate a taboo, imitate a threat signal, or create an accidental territorial
claim. Isolationists may ignore messages, jam them, answer through intermediaries or demand withdrawal.
No single failed phrase should mechanically force war; escalation should pass through observable
decisions, fear, military posture and available ways to clarify the incident.

## Economy and exchange

During unification, former national currencies were consolidated into a Commonwealth digital fiat
currency that retains the familiar **dollar ($)** name. Dollars govern discretionary exchange,
investment and accounting while necessities are guaranteed through the Commonwealth's universal
provision system. Other civilizations begin with institutions suited to their own histories: fiat
currency, commodity accounts, obligation ledgers, energy allocations or other systems. Interstellar
trade initially uses contracts, clearing arrangements and negotiated measures.

The **Universal Credit** should emerge later as a digital settlement standard backed by a network of
participating institutions. It is valuable because parties agree how disputes, exchange and reserves
work—not because the galaxy accepts a magical currency. Civilizations may refuse it, manipulate its
governance or operate competing credit networks.

## Narrative structure

Stellar Continuum supports two related experiences.

### Story Campaign — The Long Dawn

A guided Human campaign uses authored characters, scenes and a selected sequence of crises while
preserving strategic choices. It teaches the 2050 opening, reaches first contact and reveals one
coherent version of the Quiet. This mode is currently shown as Coming Soon and is not implemented.

### Sandbox — Chronicle events

Sandbox uses **story situations**, not a fixed plot. Each situation has simulation triggers, information
requirements, involved populations, choices and persistent consequences. The same premise can resolve
differently because geography, species, governments and prior history differ by seed.

Events should be written from an in-world source—scientific report, diplomatic exchange, testimony,
news summary, intercepted message or later historical account. The source may be incomplete. Player
choices should operate through real resources, orders, laws, deployments or agreements where those
systems exist.

## Canon and uncertainty labels

Future lore records should label claims as:

- **Observed fact:** supported by the current observer's legitimate measurements.
- **Accepted account:** treated as true by an institution but not fully verified.
- **Disputed account:** credible sources conflict.
- **Rumor or hypothesis:** possible, actionable and explicitly uncertain.
- **Author-only truth:** hidden campaign state; never exposed before discovery.

This vocabulary lets the game contain deep lore without using an omniscient encyclopedia.

## Naming rules

- Species names and self-names may differ from Human translations.
- Civilization names describe political institutions, not entire species.
- Avoid giving every alien an apostrophe-heavy name or a single cultural trait.
- Name technologies by what the civilization understands, then allow names to change as understanding
  improves or foreign origins become known.
- Places can accumulate names from discoverers, inhabitants, governments and later historians.

## Implementation boundary

This lore foundation does not yet add event runtime, story characters, language research, a leader
system, military branches, Universal Credits, named Thalori gameplay, insectoid biology or the Chorus.
Those items require explicit mechanics, observer-safe presentation, persistence and validation before
they are described as implemented.
